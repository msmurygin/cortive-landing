 <section id="cost-drivers"><h2>Compare the <em>whole scope</em></h2><div class="q-mod-cols" style="margin:0"><div class="q-stack"><h3>Licensing and subscription</h3><p>A subscription and a perpetual licence have different payment profiles. Check how users, sites, modules, environments, upgrades and support are charged. “Cloud” describes a deployment model; it does not by itself define the price or who operates the system.</p></div><div class="q-stack"><h3>Implementation and integrations</h3><p>Budget for process mapping, configuration, testing and cutover. For each ERP, carrier, automation or marketplace connection, identify the interface owner, error handling and ongoing maintenance. Internal delivery still uses people’s time; include it explicitly.</p></div><div class="q-stack"><h3>Migration, RF devices and infrastructure</h3><p>Account for master-data cleanup, opening balances and reconciliation. Check scanners, printers, labels, wireless coverage, charging, replacement devices and any hosting or local infrastructure. Hardware compatibility must be evaluated against the selected WMS.</p></div><div class="q-stack"><h3>Training, transition and support</h3><p>Include operator training, supervisor preparation, temporary parallel processes, support coverage and future changes. Ask what the proposed implementation and annual support actually include.</p></div></div></section>

 <div class="q-notice"><strong>How the calculation works</strong><p>Initial cost is the sum of one-off software, implementation days × day rate, warehouse setup, integrations, migration, training and devices/infrastructure, plus your contingency percentage on that sum. Annual recurring cost is twelve months of subscription fees, if selected, plus annual support and other annual operating costs. TCO = initial cost + years × annual recurring cost. Recurring costs begin in year one.</p></div><p class="q-note">Your scope and financial inputs stay in this page’s memory. They are not stored, sent to analytics or included in contact requests.</p></section>
 <section id="configurability"><h2>Why configurability matters <em>to ownership costs</em></h2><p>A warehouse changes after go-live. Ask which process changes can be handled through supported configuration and which need custom code. For example, <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/warehousing/control-warehouse-location-directives">Microsoft documents configurable work templates and location directives</a> for warehouse operations. Its <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/guidance/implementation-guide/implementation-strategy">implementation guidance</a> also explains that extensions add maintenance and support costs beyond the initial build.</p><p>For a WMS evaluation, this means comparing the work needed to configure, document, test and maintain changes across upgrades. Configuration still needs ownership and testing. Ask each supplier to demonstrate a representative process change and explain the ongoing responsibilities. This is a cost-planning consideration, not evidence of Quark-specific savings or a claim that Quark supports those particular configuration tools.</p></section>
+<section id="compare-quotes"><h2>Compare WMS quotes <em>on the same scope</em></h2><p>Ask each supplier to separate one-off and recurring charges against the same warehouses, users, processes and connections. Record whether each item is included, separately priced, excluded or still unknown. An unknown cost is not zero.</p><ol class="q-checks"><li><span><strong>Software and access.</strong> Confirm the charging unit, included sites, users, modules and environments. Ask what changes when your operation grows.</span></li><li><span><strong>Implementation and acceptance.</strong> List configuration, migration, training, testing and cutover responsibilities. Ask which deliverables and acceptance checks the estimate covers.</span></li><li><span><strong>Integrations.</strong> Name each connection and its owner. Separate build and testing from subscriptions, monitoring and ongoing maintenance.</span></li><li><span><strong>Support and future changes.</strong> Confirm support coverage, hosting, upgrades and regression testing. Identify work your own team must budget for.</span></li><li><span><strong>Commercial assumptions.</strong> Record currency, tax treatment, renewal terms, price changes and exclusions. Use the same comparison period and avoid counting bundled items twice.</span></li></ol><p class="q-note">The calculator models one recurring annual amount over three or five years. It does not model changing user counts, annual price increases, tax/VAT, exchange rates or financing. Assess those separately when comparing actual proposals.</p><p>Use <a href="/product#inbound">receiving and putaway</a>, <a href="/product#inventory">inventory control</a> and <a href="/product#execution">warehouse execution</a> to define the operations you want to discuss with Quark. Customer-specific connections and changes need their own agreed scope.</p></section>
 <section id="overruns"><h2>Where budgets can <em>become incomplete</em></h2><p>Unclear process scope, poor master data, late interface changes, insufficient user testing and unplanned cutover work can expand a project. Ask who owns each item and whether it is included in the estimate. A contingency is an assumption, not a guarantee that an overrun will fit inside it.</p></section>
 <section id="roi"><h2>Keep cost and <em>ROI separate</em></h2><p>This calculator estimates costs from your inputs. It does not estimate savings or predict ROI. A business case needs a measured baseline and defensible benefits over the same period. ROI = (benefits − costs) / costs when costs are greater than zero. Payback is a separate timing measure. Avoid counting the same released capacity as both a cash saving and extra revenue without evidence.</p></section>
