HOW THE MODULES CONNECT

Operations share inventory and execution context.

The capability map connects warehouse processes with task execution, configuration and external systems.

01 / INBOUND

From receipt to a location.

A receipt is only useful when the stock identity and its destination can be followed.

Capability scope

  • Receiving goods and recording receipts
  • Putaway destinations and stock placement

What to evaluate

  1. Confirm the received quantity and stock identity.
  2. Review the destination and check the resulting stock.

02 / INVENTORY

Understand stock in context.

A quantity alone does not explain where stock is, its identity or why it is held.

Capability scope

  • Stock balances and movement records
  • Holds, lots, serial numbers and containers
  • Inventory counts and stock adjustments
  • Replenishment planning and execution

What to evaluate

  1. Inspect the location, owner, SKU and relevant stock identities.
  2. Relate a balance or hold to its operation history.
  3. Review count, adjustment and replenishment requirements.

03 / OUTBOUND

Connect the order to execution.

Allocation, picking and shipping need clear handoffs and visible exceptions.

Capability scope

  • Allocation and stock reservation
  • Picking review and completion
  • Packing workstations and carton completion
  • Dispatch and loading

What to evaluate

  1. Examine the demand, reservation and remaining quantity.
  2. Follow completion and the handoff to the next operation.
  3. Define the checks between loading and goods issue.

04 / EXECUTION

Give each action a clear context.

Operators and supervisors need to know which task they are doing and what happens when it cannot be completed.

Capability scope

  • RF terminal workflows
  • Task ownership, progress and handoffs

What to evaluate

  1. Review the operator journey on the relevant RF interface.
  2. Check task ownership, progress and exception handling.

05 / PLATFORM

Evaluate how the setup changes.

A process change needs an identifiable owner, a clear version and a checked operational effect.

Capability scope

  • Metadata Studio and configuration management
  • Report definitions and templates
  • Integration-event visibility

What to evaluate

  1. Ask to compare one supported configuration change before and after use.
  2. Check a report output against its source data.
  3. Confirm interface ownership, error handling and recovery.

These examples help frame an evaluation. They describe questions and checks to work through together, rather than a self-service product demonstration.

Investigate a stock balance.

A supervisor needs to understand why stock in a location is unavailable.

Warehouse goal
Identify the stock and explain the state that the operator sees.
What to check
A clear set of facts to discuss: which stock is involved, what happened and what still needs investigation.

EVALUATION STEPS

  1. Step 1Find the relevant owner, SKU, location and lot or container.
  2. Step 2Review the displayed hold context and relevant movement history.
  3. Step 3Check that the recorded operation explains the balance.

Review demand and allocation.

An order has partially allocated demand. A further allocation needs to be understood.

Warehouse goal
Compare the reserved stock and remaining demand before and after the operation.
What to check
An explicit reservation change and any remaining demand. Picking and shipping are separate steps to examine.

EVALUATION STEPS

  1. Step 1Review the demand and its current reservation.
  2. Step 2Follow the agreed allocation scenario in the available build.
  3. Step 3Compare the resulting reservation and remaining demand.

Evaluate a process change.

A warehouse process needs a change in its supported configuration.

Warehouse goal
Understand the change, who owns it and how its effect is checked.
What to check
An agreed before/after evaluation, with responsibilities and any development needs made clear.

EVALUATION STEPS

  1. Step 1Describe the current operation and expected difference.
  2. Step 2Identify the supported setting, its version and the steps needed to apply it.
  3. Step 3Repeat the same operation, compare the result and discuss how to restore the prior setup.
