Quark WMS/Warehouse Inventory Management

Warehouse inventory management for daily operations

Understand what stock you hold, where it is and which operations changed it. Bring stock visibility, movements, holds, counting and adjustments into the same warehouse conversation.

Quark WMS is an early-stage product. Its inventory scope covers the workflows below; an evaluation of the selected build establishes the processes, configuration and devices that fit your warehouse.

  • Inventory visibility
  • Movements and stock records
  • Holds, lots and containers
  • Counting and adjustments
  • Plan an evaluation

Find inventory in its warehouse context

Review stock by SKU, owner, location, lot and container. Inventory views represent physical, allocated and picked quantities, with availability and hold context available through the stock read model. Search fields include location, SKU, owner, lot, LPN and serial.

This context helps an operations team ask a specific question about an item or location, instead of treating the warehouse as a single stock total.

Explore the existing inventory screens.

Connect movements with stock records

The inventory scope includes stock movement, receipt deposits and movement records. Review the source, destination, quantity and operational context needed to understand how goods reached their current position.

Putaway, picking and replenishment connect to the wider WMS scope. Agree which connected steps belong in your evaluation and test their handoffs with representative data.

Inspect the movement-record interface.

Keep holds, lots and containers visible

Hold workflows include placing, inspecting and releasing holds on a lot, location or movable unit. Lot, container and serial context make it possible to describe the stock involved in an operation more precisely.

Rules for expiry, serial capture, container structure, ownership and permitted stock use depend on the selected workflow and configuration. Review those requirements against the available build before agreeing on a production scope.

Count stock and review differences

The counting scope includes location tasks, observations, empty-location confirmation, recounts and variance review. Cycle-count policies provide a way to define which locations should be counted. Counting and correcting stock are distinct steps.

Controlled inventory adjustments

Adjustment documents support target quantities, reason codes, validation, approval where required, posting and reversal paths. Count variances connect to the adjustment workflow so the physical observation and the decision to change stock can be reviewed separately.

Make accuracy measurable

Define the stock identity, expected quantity, counted quantity and acceptable difference before measuring inventory accuracy. Use a consistent baseline and investigate the movement or adjustment behind a variance. This page promises no accuracy percentage, savings or automatic elimination of discrepancies.

Evaluate the complete operational scope

Bring receiving, storage, stock movements, holds, counts and outbound handoffs into the evaluation where they matter to your operation. Include the normal path, an exception, the roles involved and the resulting stock and records.

  • Which inventory identities and ownership boundaries must be maintained?
  • Which movements, holds and counting rules apply to your stock?
  • Which devices and systems take part in the process?
  • Who can confirm a variance or approve an adjustment?
  • What must be demonstrated before production use?

Evaluation and implementation scope are agreed individually. AI Harness, AI Onboarding and AI Support remain long-term concepts.
